Part 1 · The Business Problem

751 Million Users. 100 Million Songs.
One Impossible Problem.

Spotify's core challenge isn't technology. It's relevance. How do you make every single listener feel like the platform was built just for them?

751MActive Users
100MSongs in Catalog
~30%Revenue from Personalization

If Spotify showed every user the same Top 50 list, it would serve casual listeners just fine. But the people who pay are the passionate music fans with specific, deeply personal taste. would leave. They would leave quietly, without filing a complaint, and Spotify would bleed revenue without knowing exactly why.

The solution is a recommendation engine: an AI system that learns what each person loves by watching what they actually do, not what they say they like. Every time you skip a song in the first five seconds, replay something three times at midnight, or let an album play all the way through, you're sending signals. Spotify collects billions of these signals every day and uses them to build a model of your taste that no survey could ever replicate.

This is the project I built in MIS 432: a simplified version of that engine, using real behavioral data, real math, and real design. to understand not just how it works technically but what it means strategically for any business that handles data at scale.

Part 2 · What I Learned

Eight Concepts That Changed
How I Think About AI

These aren't just technical terms. Each one is a business decision in disguise.

👆
Implicit Signals
Spotify doesn't ask you to rate songs. It watches whether you skip, replay, or let something play. Behavior is more honest than opinion because you cannot fake what your finger actually does.
⚙️
Feature Engineering
Raw data doesn't teach machines anything. I transformed listening events into structured numbers. skip rate, replay rate, and genre affinity that a model could actually learn from. The quality of your features determines the quality of your AI.
🤝
Collaborative Filtering
Find people who listen like you, then recommend what they loved that you haven't heard yet. No genre labels needed. No human curation. Just math applied to behavior, running across 751 million users simultaneously.
🔍
Unsupervised Learning
No one told the algorithm what "late-night chill" means. It found that cluster on its own by noticing that certain behavioral patterns. long sessions, low skip rates, slow tempos. kept appearing together: long sessions, low skip rates, slow tempos, late hours.
📐
Embeddings
Spotify converts every song and every user into a point in mathematical space. Songs that "sound alike" end up near each other. Users with similar taste end up near each other. Proximity becomes prediction.
⚠️
Training-Serving Gap
The model learned from last quarter's data. But music trends change weekly. When the model goes stale, nothing crashes. Recommendations just quietly get worse. Silent failure is the most dangerous kind because no alarm ever sounds.
🏛️
AI Governance
When a stale model silently harms user experience for weeks, that is not a technical failure. It is a governance failure. Someone needed a monitoring process, an alert threshold, a review cycle. AI governance is a management problem, not a coding problem.
🏰
The Data Moat
A competitor can copy Spotify's app overnight. They cannot manufacture ten years of 751 million people honestly revealing their taste through behavior. That behavioral history is the real product. The moat widens every day Spotify operates.
Part 3 · The Wrapped Insight

Why Spotify Wrapped Costs
Almost Nothing to Produce

Every December, Spotify generates 250 million personalized year-in-review summaries that go viral. The engineering cost is nearly zero. Here's why.

Spotify Wrapped isn't a separate product. It's a query on top of infrastructure that already exists. The behavioral data that powers your recommendations, every skip, replay, and session length, is the same data that generates your Wrapped. The pipeline is already running. Wrapped is just a different way of reading it.

This is one of the most important lessons in data strategy: a well-built data infrastructure is not a cost center. It is a compounding asset. The same investment that makes recommendations more accurate also makes marketing campaigns more targeted, makes artist royalty calculations more precise, and makes Wrapped a billion-dollar viral moment that costs almost nothing to execute.

In this project, I built a Wrapped feature using the exact same engagement scores that powered the recommendation engine. No new data. No separate system. One pipeline, multiple use cases, just like the real thing.

Part 4 · Business Takeaways

3 Lessons for Any Business
Building with AI

01
The data you already have is your most underused asset.
Most businesses collect behavioral signals without realizing it. click patterns, purchase sequences, and support ticket language. Spotify did not invent new data. It built systems that made existing data useful at scale. Any business that handles customer behavior is sitting on the same raw material.
02
Silent failure is more dangerous than visible failure.
A crashed system demands immediate attention. A quietly degrading recommendation model slowly erodes user trust until people stop coming back. By the time you notice, the damage is already done. AI governance means building systems that fail loudly, not just systems that seem to work.
03
Personalization is not a feature. It is a retention strategy.
The users who drive recurring revenue are not the casual ones who would be fine with a Top 50 list. They are the passionate ones who will leave the moment the product stops understanding them. Building AI that serves individual taste at scale is not a nice-to-have. It is the business model.
